Every log line can also leave the service as an `egradx.log.record.v1` event, over the [events transport](/docs/guides/events). Whatever stores your logs handles that event type. In [the Initializr](/start?caps=events,log-shipping), choose **Log shipping**.

## 2. Use a broker

With the in-process transport, the lines go to this service's own handlers. If none of them handles `egradx.log.record.v1`, the lines go nowhere. Ship over [RabbitMQ or Kafka](/docs/recipes/move-to-rabbitmq).

## 3. Store them

A small service of your own, on the same SDK, handles `egradx.log.record.v1` and writes the lines to whatever stores your logs (OpenSearch, Loki, a table). Each record carries the level, logger, message, stack trace, service, trace id, request id, person and organization. The [events reference](/docs/reference/events) has the exact fields.

## What it costs

- Logging only queues the line. A background sender ships batches straight over the transport, **not** through the outbox, so a log line never waits on the database.
- A full queue drops lines and counts them. Shipping is best effort.
- The transports' own loggers are never shipped (no loops). Add your own noisy loggers to `excluded`.

| | Java (`egradx.observability.logging.shipping.*`) | Node / Python | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lowest level | `minimum-level` | `minimumLevel` / `minimum_level` | INFO |
| Queue, batch, flush | `queue-capacity`, `batch-size`, `flush-interval` | `queueCapacity`, `batchSize`, `flushIntervalMs` / `queue_capacity`, `batch_size`, `flush_interval` | 10 000, 200, 1 s |
