## What it is

The SDK decides **who** is calling and **what they hold**; your application decides **what each endpoint needs**. A permission is an *authority*: a string such as `note.write` that your code names, an administrator puts on roles, and the SDK resolves for every request.

- Check **authorities, never role names.** Roles belong to each organization's administrators; they can be renamed or reshaped at any time.
- **Every endpoint states what it needs.** The SDK lets every request through to your code — signed in or not — so an endpoint without a check is public.
- A frontend can hide a button for those who lack an authority, but only the server's check counts.

## What callers get

<RecordedRun name="first-endpoint" steps={['without-token', 'without-permission', 'created']} />

| Situation | Answer |
|---|---|
| No token | 401 `NOT_AUTHENTICATED` |
| A token sent but refused (expired, sessions ended, organization suspended) | 401 `SESSION_EXPIRED`: renew it, or sign in again |
| Signed in, without the authority | 403 `ACCESS_DENIED` |

The browser client renews the token on a 401 by itself and gives up on a refusal of the renewal (`SESSION_REVOKED`); a coded 403 is a decision, never retried.

## Your application's permissions

Authorities live in a catalog in the SDK's tables. Your application adds its own once, at start-up — creating one that exists fails, hence "list, then create what is missing" — and gives them to roles.

- **Names:** nothing is enforced; the name stored is the one you pass. Keep them `area.action` (`note.write`, `order.cancel`). The `egradx.` prefix is the SDK's own.
- **Who holds them:** the listener on *organization created* runs in the provisioning transaction, so a new organization's roles hold your authorities before anyone signs in.
- **Organizations that already exist** when you add a new authority are not given it automatically: add it to their roles yourself (`addAuthoritiesToRole` for each organization), or let their administrators do it.
- **Roles of your own** (Manager, Staff…): create them in the same listener — `createSystemRole(organizationId, name, description, authorities)` makes one its administrators cannot delete or rename.

## Checking in code

Some decisions depend on the request — "cancelling needs `order.cancel`, other status changes `order.create`". Check the caller's authorities where you decide:

| | Java | Node | Python |
|---|---|---|---|
| The caller's authorities | `tenant.current().orElseThrow().hasAuthority("order.cancel")` | `currentCaller()!.authorities.has('order.cancel')` | `"order.cancel" in current_caller().authorities` |
| Refuse | `throw new AuthorizationException("…")` → 403 `ACCESS_DENIED` | same | same |

## The SDK's own authorities

| Authority | Lets its holder | In every organization's `Admin` |
|---|---|---|
| `egradx.user.read`, `egradx.user.manage` | see, and manage, the organization's people and invitations | yes |
| `egradx.role.read`, `egradx.role.manage` | see, and manage, its roles | yes |
| `egradx.audit.read` | read and export its audit trail | yes |
| `egradx.notification.read`, `egradx.notification.manage` | see, and set up, its channels, templates and delivery log | yes |
| `egradx.organization.read`, `.manage`, `egradx.audit.platform.read`, `egradx.notification.broadcast` | the same across every organization | no: the platform organization only |

[The permissions reference](/docs/reference/permissions) lists them with their descriptions.

## Guarantees and limits

- **Checked against now:** the role a person holds and what it contains are read on every request, so a change applies to their next request (up to 60 seconds later on other instances, where a role's authorities are cached).
- **Someone removed from an organization** keeps a session there, holding nothing, until it ends. Gate every route on an authority, not on "signed in".
- **The platform administrator** holds none of your authorities.
- **The frontend:** `<Can authority="note.write">` and `hasAuthority(...)` decide what to show from the session's snapshot. They are never the check.
